What is a meta tag generator?

    
A meta tag generator builds the small pieces of HTML that go in the <head> of a web page and describe that page to search engines and social platforms. Getting them right is one of the quickest on-page SEO wins there is — but writing them by hand is fiddly, and a single typo in an Open Graph tag can break how your link looks everywhere it's shared. This tool writes them for you and shows a live preview of exactly how your page will appear in Google and on social media before you ship a single line.


    
The meta tags that actually matter

    
Title tag

    
The <title> is the single most important on-page SEO element after your content. It's the clickable blue headline in Google and the label on your browser tab. Aim for roughly 50–60 characters, lead with your main keyword, and keep it unique on every page.

    
Meta description

    
The meta description is the grey snippet under your title in search results. It is not a ranking factor — Google has confirmed this repeatedly — but it heavily influences whether people click. Treat it as ad copy: around 150–160 characters, benefit-led, with a reason to click. If you leave it blank, Google will pull a snippet from your page instead.

    
Canonical

    
The canonical tag tells search engines which URL is the "real" one when the same content is reachable at several addresses. It prevents duplicate-content dilution and consolidates ranking signals onto one URL.

    
Open Graph & Twitter Cards

    
These control the title, description and image shown when your link is shared on Facebook, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Slack, Discord and X. Without them, platforms guess — often badly. With a proper og:image (1200×630), your links look professional and get clicked more.


    
How to use the generated tags

    
Click Generate, copy the output, and paste it inside the <head>…</head> section of your page's HTML — ideally near the top, before any scripts. If you use WordPress, an SEO plugin like Yoast or Rank Math exposes the same fields; you can paste the values straight in. After publishing, re-share the URL or use a social debugger to refresh the cached preview.

Frequently asked questions

    

      
Does the meta description help my ranking?
No. It isn't a ranking factor. It affects click-through rate only — a good description earns more clicks, but it won't move your position directly.

      
How long should my title and description be?
Roughly 50–60 characters for the title and 150–160 for the description, so they aren't cut off. Google actually measures pixel width, so the counters above are a guide, not a hard rule.

      
Do I need Open Graph tags?
If you care how your links look when shared on social platforms, yes. Open Graph controls the title, description and image on Facebook, LinkedIn and most apps; Twitter Card tags do the same on X.
