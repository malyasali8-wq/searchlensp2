How to optimize for AI Overviews & ChatGPT search

Guide · Updated 2026-10-07

AI answers now sit on top of search. Here's how to make your content the thing they pull from — and cite.

The short version: AI answer engines pick sources that are clear, trustworthy and easy to extract. Win by writing answer-first content, marking it up with structured data, establishing strong entities and authorship, keeping key facts in plain HTML, and letting AI crawlers access your site.

What are AI Overviews and AI search?

Google's AI Overviews are the AI-generated summaries that now appear at the top of many search results, pulling from several web pages and often linking a few. ChatGPT search, Perplexity and Claude work similarly: they read multiple sources and compose an answer, usually citing some of them. Being one of those cited sources is the new prize — it drives visibility and clicks even in a "zero-click" world.

1. Write answer-first content

Lead with the answer. If someone asks "how long should a title tag be?", the first sentence should say "around 50–60 characters" — then explain. AI engines extract clean, self-contained answers; burying the point three paragraphs down means it may be skipped. Use the inverted-pyramid style: conclusion first, detail after.

2. Structure for extraction

Use descriptive, question-style headings (H2/H3) that mirror how people ask. Keep paragraphs tight. Use lists and tables for steps and comparisons — they're easy for models to lift. Make sure the important facts are in plain HTML text, not inside images, PDFs or rendered only by JavaScript.

3. Add structured data

Structured data helps engines understand exactly what your content is. FAQPage, Article and Organization schema are especially useful — FAQ markup maps directly to question/answer pairs that AI loves, and Organization/Author markup helps establish who is behind the content.

4. Build entity clarity and authority

AI engines favour sources they can trust. Make it obvious who you are: a clear author or organization, an About page, consistent naming, and links to authoritative references. Cover a topic thoroughly across related pages (topical authority) rather than one thin post. Earn mentions and links from reputable sites.

5. Keep content fresh

Freshness is a signal for AI answers, particularly on fast-moving topics. Update key pages, show a visible "last updated" date, and keep facts current. Stale content is less likely to be chosen.

6. Let AI crawlers in

If AI engines can't crawl you, they can't cite you. Check your robots.txt allows the major AI crawlers (GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended) unless you deliberately want to opt out. Then publish an llms.txt file that lists and describes your most important pages, giving models a clean map of your site.

7. Measure and iterate

Track whether you appear in AI Overviews and AI tools for your key queries. Note which pages get cited and why, and double down on that format. Run a page-level audit to catch gaps.

Common mistakes

  • Answer hidden deep in the page instead of up top.
  • Key content locked behind JavaScript or inside images.
  • No structured data, so engines must guess.
  • AI crawlers accidentally blocked in robots.txt.
  • Thin, one-off posts with no topical depth or authority.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get cited in Google AI Overviews?

Write clear answer-first content, add Article and FAQ structured data, keep key facts in plain HTML, build topical authority and authorship signals, and make sure Google can crawl the page. Being a trusted, extractable source is what gets you cited.

Does blocking AI crawlers hurt my visibility?

Yes, if you want AI visibility. If GPTBot, ClaudeBot or PerplexityBot are blocked in robots.txt, those engines can't read or cite your content. Allow them unless you specifically want to opt out of AI.

What is an llms.txt file and does it help?

llms.txt is a Markdown file at your site root that points AI models to your most important pages with short descriptions. It's a low-effort, growing standard that makes your best content easier for AI to find and cite.

Is AI search killing SEO traffic?

It changes it. More answers happen without a click, but being the cited source still drives qualified visits, and interactive tools and deep content still earn clicks. Optimizing for AI is now part of SEO, not a replacement.
