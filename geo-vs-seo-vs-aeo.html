GEO vs SEO vs AEO: what's the difference?

Guide · Updated 2026-10-07

Three acronyms, one goal — getting found. Here's what SEO, AEO and GEO actually mean, how they overlap, and what to do about each.

In short: SEO gets you ranked in traditional search results. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) gets you into direct answers and featured snippets. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) gets your content understood and cited by AI engines like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude. They overlap heavily — strong SEO is the foundation for all three.

SEO — Search Engine Optimization

SEO is the original discipline: optimizing your pages so they rank well in search engines like Google and Bing. It covers on-page factors (titles, headings, content, internal links), technical health (crawlability, speed, mobile, structured data) and off-page signals (backlinks and authority). The goal is a high position in the classic list of blue links so people click through to your site.

SEO is not going away. Even as AI answers grow, the underlying signals that make a page rank — clear content, good structure, authority — are the same signals AI systems rely on to decide what to trust and cite.

AEO — Answer Engine Optimization

AEO is about winning the direct answer, not just the ranking. Think of Google's featured snippets, the "People also ask" boxes, and voice-assistant answers. Instead of only ranking a page, you structure your content so a search engine can lift a clean, correct answer straight out of it.

The tactics are concrete: lead with the answer, then explain (answer-first writing); use clear question-style headings; keep key facts in plain text, not buried in images or scripts; and add FAQ and other structured data so engines can parse your Q&A. AEO is essentially SEO tuned for extraction.

GEO — Generative Engine Optimization

GEO is the newest layer: optimizing so that generative AI engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews, Claude — understand, trust and cite your content when they compose an answer. These systems don't just rank pages; they read many sources and synthesize a response, often naming a few of them.

GEO builds on SEO and AEO and adds a few AI-specific moves: making sure your best content is in clean, extractable HTML (not locked behind scripts); establishing clear entities and authorship; keeping content fresh; allowing AI crawlers in your robots.txt; and publishing an llms.txt file that points AI models to your most important pages.

Do you have to pick one?

No — and you shouldn't. They're layers on the same cake. If you do solid SEO, write answer-first content, add structured data, and open the door to AI crawlers, you're covering all three at once. The mistake is treating GEO and AEO as totally separate projects; in practice they're a handful of extra habits on top of good SEO.

A simple starting checklist

  • Write a clear, keyword-aligned title and meta description for every page.
  • Answer the main question in the first paragraph, then expand.
  • Use question-style H2/H3 headings for sub-topics.
  • Add structured data (Organization, Article, FAQ).
  • Allow AI crawlers in robots.txt and publish an llms.txt.
  • Keep important content in plain HTML text, and keep it fresh.

Frequently asked questions

Is GEO replacing SEO?

No. GEO builds on SEO. The signals that help you rank — clear content, structure and authority — are the same signals AI engines use to decide what to cite. Do SEO first, then layer GEO and AEO on top.

What is the difference between AEO and GEO?

AEO targets direct answers inside traditional search (featured snippets, voice, People Also Ask). GEO targets generative AI engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, AI Overviews) that synthesize and cite sources. They share most tactics.

How do I optimize for ChatGPT and Perplexity?

Keep your best content in clean, extractable HTML, establish clear entities and authorship, allow AI crawlers in robots.txt, publish an llms.txt file, and write answer-first content. See our guides and free tools.

Which should a small site focus on first?

Start with core SEO (titles, content, structure, speed). Then add answer-first writing and FAQ schema (AEO), and open AI-crawler access plus llms.txt (GEO). It's a progression, not a choice.
