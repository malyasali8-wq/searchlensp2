What is a robots.txt file?

    
robots.txt is a plain-text file at the root of your website — reachable at yourdomain.com/robots.txt — that tells crawlers which parts of your site they may or may not request. It's the first file most bots look at. Well-behaved crawlers (Googlebot, Bingbot, and the major AI crawlers) respect it; it is a set of directives, not a security wall, so it won't stop a determined bad actor.

If you want AI visibility (as most publishers now do), allow them — being citable in ChatGPT, Perplexity and AI Overviews is a growing traffic source. If you'd rather keep your content out of AI, block them. This tool lets you choose each one.


    
How Disallow and Allow work

    
Each block starts with a User-agent: line, then one or more Disallow: or Allow: paths. Disallow: / blocks everything; Disallow: (empty) blocks nothing. When rules conflict, most modern crawlers follow the longest (most specific) matching path. Our tester uses the same longest-match logic so you can check a path before you ship.


    
Important: robots.txt does not deindex

    
Blocking a URL with Disallow stops crawling, but if other sites link to that URL it can still show up in Google without a description. To actually remove a page from the index, allow crawling and add a <meta name="robots" content="noindex"> tag to the page instead.


    
Frequently asked questions

    

      
Where does robots.txt go?
At the root of your domain, reachable at yourdomain.com/robots.txt. A robots.txt inside a subfolder is ignored.

      
Should I block AI crawlers?
Only if you don't want your content used by or cited in AI. Most sites now allow them for the AI-search visibility.

      
Does Disallow remove a page from Google?
No — it stops crawling but the URL can still be indexed if linked. Use a noindex meta tag to deindex.
