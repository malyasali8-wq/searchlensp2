SEO audit checklist for 2026

Guide · Updated 2026-10-07

A practical, no-fluff checklist across on-page, technical and AI-search readiness — plus the free tool to fix each one.

How to use this: work top to bottom on one page at a time. Or let SearchLens scan the page and flag these automatically with ready-to-paste fixes.

On-page SEO

  • Title tag — unique, ~50–60 characters, main keyword near the front. Generate it.
  • Meta description — ~150–160 characters, compelling (affects clicks, not ranking).
  • One H1 — a single, descriptive H1 that matches the page intent.
  • Heading structure — logical H2/H3, ideally question-style for AEO.
  • Keyword alignment — content actually answers the target query.
  • Image alt text — descriptive alt on meaningful images.
  • Internal links — link to and from related pages with clear anchor text.
  • Readable URLs — short, lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-relevant.

Technical SEO

  • Indexability — the page isn't accidentally noindex or blocked.
  • Canonical tag — points to the correct, absolute URL.
  • robots.txt — present, correct, not blocking what should be crawled. Check/build it.
  • XML sitemap — exists, lists canonical URLs, submitted to Search Console. Generate it.
  • HTTPS — secure, no mixed content.
  • Mobile-friendly — responsive, readable, tappable.
  • Core Web Vitals — reasonable LCP, CLS and interaction latency.
  • No broken links — internal and outbound links resolve.

Structured data & entities

  • Organization/Website schema site-wide. Generate it.
  • Article/Product/FAQ schema where relevant.
  • Valid markup — passes Google's Rich Results Test.
  • Clear authorship — who wrote/owns the content.

AI-search readiness (GEO / AEO)

  • AI crawlers allowed — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended not blocked (unless intended). Guide.
  • llms.txt present — a curated map for AI models. Generate it.
  • Answer-first content — the main answer is near the top.
  • Extractable — key content is in plain HTML, not images or JS-only.
  • Freshness — important pages are up to date with a visible date.

Turn the checklist into action

A checklist is only useful if you act on it. Frequently asked questions

How often should I run an SEO audit?

Do a quick page-level check whenever you publish or significantly edit a page, and a fuller site audit every quarter, or after any redesign, migration or ranking drop.

What's the difference between an on-page and technical audit?

On-page covers content and HTML signals (titles, headings, copy, internal links). Technical covers crawlability, indexing, speed, mobile, HTTPS and structured data. A complete audit includes both, plus AI-search readiness.

Do I need paid tools to audit a page?

No. You can cover the essentials with free tools — SearchLens for the full page scan, plus free generators for meta tags, schema, robots.txt, sitemap and llms.txt to fix what it finds.

What's new in SEO audits for 2026?

AI-search readiness: whether AI crawlers can access you, whether you publish an llms.txt, and whether your content is answer-first and extractable so engines like ChatGPT and AI Overviews can cite it.
