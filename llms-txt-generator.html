What is an llms.txt file?

    
llms.txt is a simple Markdown file you place at the root of your website — reachable at yourdomain.com/llms.txt, exactly like robots.txt. Its job is to give large language models a clean, curated map of your most important content, written in a format they can read instantly.

    
Search is changing fast. A growing share of people now get answers from AI engines — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews, Claude — instead of clicking through a page of blue links. Those engines don't read your site the way a human does; they pull and cite specific passages. An llms.txt file makes their job easier by pointing them straight to your best pages, with a short description of each, so your content is more likely to be understood and cited correctly.


    
Why llms.txt matters for AI search (GEO / AEO)

    
Modern websites are heavy — navigation, scripts, banners, pop-ups. When an AI model tries to understand your site, most of that is noise. llms.txt cuts through it: a single, human-written file that says "here is what this site is, and here are the pages that matter." This is a core part of Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) and Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) — the practice of getting your content surfaced and cited by AI answer engines.

    
It won't replace good content, a sitemap, or solid technical SEO — think of it as an extra, AI-friendly layer on top. But it's cheap to add, takes minutes, and puts you ahead of the many sites that still don't have one.


    
The llms.txt format (what this generator produces)

    
A valid llms.txt file follows a simple structure:

    
    
      
  • An H1 title — your site or project name (# Acme Analytics).
    • 
      
  • A blockquote summary — one line describing what the site is (> ...).
    • 
      
  • One or more section headings (## Pages) followed by a list of Markdown links, each with an optional short description after a colon.

That's it. Because it's plain Markdown, it stays readable to both humans and machines. This tool builds that structure for you automatically — you just fill in the details.


    
How to use your llms.txt file

    
Once you've generated it: copy or download the file, name it exactly llms.txt, and upload it to the root folder of your website so it's reachable at yourdomain.com/llms.txt. If you use Vercel, Netlify or a static host, drop it beside your homepage file. Then confirm it loads by visiting the URL in your browser. You can update it any time you add important pages.


    
llms.txt vs robots.txt vs sitemap.xml

    
They're three different files that work together. robots.txt tells crawlers what they may or may not access. sitemap.xml lists every URL for search engines to crawl. llms.txt is the opposite of a full list — it's a short, hand-picked, described set of your best content, written for AI models. Having all three is ideal.


    
Frequently asked questions

    

      
Where exactly do I put the llms.txt file?
At the root of your domain, so it opens at yourdomain.com/llms.txt — the same place your robots.txt lives.

      
Is llms.txt the same as robots.txt?
No. robots.txt restricts what crawlers can access; llms.txt highlights and describes your best content so AI models can find and cite it.

      
Do AI engines actually use it?
Adoption is growing across the AI-search ecosystem, and it's a low-effort, low-risk addition. Even where it isn't consumed yet, it does no harm and future-proofs your site.
